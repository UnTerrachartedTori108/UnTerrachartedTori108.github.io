Activity 1, Link 2 (TutorialsPoint: Internet Technologies) Describe: Unlike GCFLearnfree, TutorialsPoint currently provides something of substance. This section, of many others, is called "Internet Overview." It defines the internet overall as "an Information superhighway, to access information over the web." Under this, it goes into more detail: The internet is a worldwide system of interconnected computer networks and is accessible to every user all over the world via their phone, computer or other devices. Each device has a unique IP address. An IP Address is a set of numbers (such as 110.22.33.114) which identifies a computer location. On top of that, "A special computer DNS (Domain Name Server) is used to give name to the IP Address so that users can locate a computer by one name." Scroll down and find the "evolution" of the internet. In other words, history. The concept of the internet was originated in 1969 by the United States Department of Defense with the concept of Advanced Research Project Agency Network (ARPANET) to provide widespread communication among the different branches of government. Though in 1972, "the ARPANET spread over the globe with 23 nodes located in different countries and thus became known as the Internet. By the time, with the invention of new technologies such as TCP/IP protocols, DNS, WWW, browsers, scripting languages etc., the Internet provided a medium to publish and access information over the web." After the history, it talks about the advantages and disadvantages. The advantages are 1. the ability to connect with people across the world, 2. gather information to learn about anything you would want, 3. access to many forms of entertainment and 4. use services for convenience, such as bank accounts or online shopping. The disadvantages are 1. the risk of personal information being leaked, 2. spam from e-mails that slows down the efficiency of the system and 3. viruses that can cause information to be leaked. Learned: I've learned about the early history of the internet and how it developed into what we all use now. I've heard of an IP address before, though i did not understand what it meant, but now I do. Envision: Being aware of how information is spread and accessed can be wise to know from a privacy angle. It is also good to be more knowledgeable about something we use every day. Activity 1, Link 3 (TutorialsPoint: Internet Connectivity) Describe: This continuous of some of the information mentioned in the Internet Overview. This section, though, is called "Internet Connectivity." It's about Internet Service Providers, otherwise known as ISPs. An ISP is a company that offers access to the internet. There are different kinds of ISPs, such as 1. Access providers; They provide access to the internet through telephone lines, cable Wi-Fi or fiber optics. 2. Mailbox Providers; Providers offer mailbox hosting services. 3. Hosting ISPs; They offer e-mail, and other web hosting services such as virtual machines, clouds. 4. Virtual ISP; ISPs offer internet access via other ISP services and 5. Free ISPs; ISPs that do not charge for internet services. There are several ways to connect to the internet. The most common are Dial-up Connection, ISDN, DSL, Cable TV, Satellite and Wireless Internet Connections. Each has a different method of getting the internet to the user's device. Learned: I didn't expect there to be so many different ways for an internet connection to be sent through different devices. It also didn't occur to me until now that there are different kinds of ISPs. Envision: It's interesting to know the technical side of how an internet connection works.
